# Deterministic automation without AI services

The owner approved implementation of conventional code alternatives for file analysis, daily giving, major donor insights, plans, tasks, and a constrained assistant. The owner reiterated that accuracy and the highest practical quality matter and that AI services must not be used in the replacement.

Implement and verify in an isolated branch only. Do not deploy, merge, change credentials, access production donor records, or run paid providers. Retain legacy implementations behind an explicit opt-in switch. Default and invalid mode settings fail closed to rules mode.

Use authentic arithmetic and dates, structured spreadsheet/text-PDF parsing, explicit unsupported OCR, tenant-scoped authorization, evidence-backed templates, read-only previews and human review for consequential actions or subjective judgments. Do not silently alter staff-evaluation weights or mislabel rules as AI. Preserve historical outputs. Never invent missing facts, currency, budgets, recipients, or assessed quality.

The implementation report must distinguish validated alternatives from release gates. Production transition remains a separate approval.

## Later clarification: minimum-cost hybrid

The owner subsequently clarified that AI may assist advisory work-quality evaluation; the goal is to minimize AI spending rather than forbid every use. Keep the deterministic path and fail-closed default. Optional evidence-based quality assistance must remain disabled until provider/data-sharing scope, activation and budget are approved. Do not enable legacy routes wholesale, change staff weights, or make autonomous personnel judgments. This checkpoint implements the deterministic foundation; bounded advisory policy is a separate in-progress step.
